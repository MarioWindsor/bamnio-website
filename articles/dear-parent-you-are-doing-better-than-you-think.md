There is a version of you that no one else sees. The one who wakes up before everyone else to prepare for the day ahead. The one who packs therapy bags, remembers appointments, fills out school forms, and celebrates tiny milestones that others might overlook. The one who worries long after everyone else has gone to sleep.

If you are that parent, this is for you.

As a mother to two boys on the autism spectrum, I have often wondered if I was doing enough. Was I making the right decisions? Should I have started therapy earlier? Am I practicing enough at home? Will my children be okay?

These questions don't come with easy answers, and I have learned that almost every parent on this journey carries them.

There Is No Perfect Parent When your child is diagnosed, it can feel as though you are suddenly expected to become an expert. You begin learning new words, attending therapy sessions, researching strategies, speaking to specialists, and trying to make the best decisions for your child. It is easy to feel as though you should always know what to do. The truth is, none of us start this journey with all the answers. We learn one day at a time, alongside our children.

Progress Isn't Always Obvious Sometimes we wait for the big milestones. The first conversation. The first friendship. The first independent skill. But growth often begins in much quieter ways. Your child may tolerate a new activity. They may try to communicate differently. They may recover from a difficult moment more quickly than before. These are not "small" achievements. They are the building blocks of bigger ones.

It's Okay to Feel Tired Parenting any child can be exhausting. Parenting a child with additional needs often asks even more of us. There are days filled with appointments, meetings, therapy sessions, school concerns, and countless decisions. There may also be moments of guilt, frustration, sadness, or uncertainty. Having these feelings does not mean you love your child any less. It means you are human.

Comparison Steals More Than Joy Every child has their own strengths. Every child has their own challenges. Every child has their own timeline. As parents, we sometimes find ourselves comparing our child's journey with someone else's. I have learned that comparison rarely brings comfort. Instead, I try to compare my children only with who they were yesterday. That is where I see the real progress.

Your Presence Matters More Than Perfection Your child will not remember whether every activity was done perfectly. They will remember that you were there. That you sat beside them. That you encouraged them. That you celebrated their efforts. That you believed in them, even on the difficult days. Love, patience, and consistency leave lasting impressions.

Give Yourself the Same Grace You Give Your Child We celebrate our children's progress because we know growth takes time. Perhaps we should offer ourselves the same kindness. Parents grow too. We become more patient. More resilient. More understanding. We learn to celebrate small victories. We learn to ask for help. And we learn that it is okay not to have all the answers.

A Gentle Reminder If today was difficult... If therapy didn't go as planned... If your child had a hard day... If you are wondering whether you are enough... Please remember this. You showed up. You loved your child. You kept going. Sometimes, that is more than enough.

One day, your child may not remember every therapy session, every worksheet, or every routine. But they will grow up knowing they had someone who never stopped believing in them. And that someone is you.

So, dear parent, if no one has told you this today - **You are doing better than you think**.
